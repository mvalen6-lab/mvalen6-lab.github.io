Is Lending Club charging enough for the risk it takes?

The question

Is a lender charging its borrowers enough interest for the risk it takes? I answered this with Lending Club's public loan data, from raw records to a decision a bank could act on.

What I did

  1. Cleaning (pandas). Started from 2.26 million loans and narrowed them to 733,102 completed loans from 2013–2015. I kept only the 14 columns known when a loan is approved, so the model couldn't “cheat” with information from the future.
  2. Analysis (SQL). Loaded the data into SQLite and compared default and interest rates by grade, loan purpose, income, state and term using GROUP BY, HAVING and aggregates.
  3. Model (scikit-learn). Built a logistic regression that predicts default with an AUC of 0.71 on unseen loans. Predicted and actual default rates match within about 1 percentage point in all 10 risk bands.
  4. Expected loss (Excel). Calculated profit per risk band with PD × LGD × exposure, with adjustable assumptions and a stress test at 60% and 80% loss given default.
  5. Dashboard (Power BI). Summarised the headline numbers and findings on one page.

Key findings

  • Risk rises much faster than price. Grade G loans default 8.9× more often than grade A loans (48.2% vs 5.4%) but pay only 3.6× the interest rate.
  • The riskiest loans earn the least. Profit per dollar lent peaks at about 10% in the middle risk bands and falls to 3.4% in the riskiest band.
  • One outlier can hide a pattern. Default and interest rates by loan purpose are strongly correlated (r = 0.88), but wedding loans alone drop that to 0.48.
Recommendation

Stop approving the riskiest band and reprice or reject the second-riskiest. Under conservative assumptions, net profit rises by 17.5% ($89.1M → $104.8M) while exposure falls by $539M.

Limits

The data comes from one U.S. lender, and the loss rates are assumptions. The recommendation should be re-tested on a bank's own portfolio before it's used.

What this shows

I can take a real financial question from 2 million rows of raw data to a recommendation a bank could act on, and explain the result to someone who didn't run the analysis.
